# The person side gauntlet: how reliable is the disposition instrument?

*DYNAMICS-WEB series, 3 September 2026. Script `cc_d8_robustness.py`. Every recent reduction tested the
character (content) side; this tests the disposition (person) side, the other half of the Ashlar bridge.
Two reliabilities decide how much weight the bridge can bear: split half (read a person's texts in two
halves, does the disposition come out the same), and two reader (do two independent model families agree
on the same texts). 58,858 persons with two or more texts; 45,502 rows read by both families.*

## The two numbers per axis

| Disposition axis | split half (within one reader) | two reader (across families) |
|---|---|---|
| discipline | 0.82 | **0.49** |
| candour | 0.79 | **0.49** |
| sociability | 0.74 | **0.48** |
| novelty | 0.69 | **0.36** |
| impulsivity | 0.82 | 0.23 |
| acuity | 0.76 | 0.20 |
| mercuriality | 0.75 | 0.08 |
| yielding | 0.76 | −0.06 |

## What it means, and it is not what the weak flag first suggests

The instrument is **internally very reliable on every axis**: split half 0.69 to 0.82 throughout. Read a
person twice and you get the same disposition. The problem is not stability.

The split is **across model families**. Four axes reproduce well when a different family reads the same
text, discipline, candour, sociability and novelty (0.36 to 0.49). Four do not: impulsivity, acuity,
mercuriality and yielding (0.23 down to −0.06). Those four are reader dependent, one family's reading of
"yielding" is not another's, so they should be treated as single reader constructs or used with that
caution stated, not presented as model independent facts.

## The part that saves the keystone

The coupling does not run on the raw eight axes; it runs on the two metatraits, plasticity (novelty and
sociability) and stability (discipline, yielding and mercuriality). Both metatraits reproduce across
families, plasticity 0.42 and stability 0.43, even though stability is built partly from the two axes the
readers most disagree on. The composite averages the reader noise out, carried by discipline. So the
person side of the bridge is sounder than the raw axis table looks, because it leans on the metatraits, and
those survive a change of reader.

## The honest bound for the person instrument

State it plainly wherever the disposition axes are used: internally reliable throughout; four axes
(discipline, candour, sociability, novelty) reproduce across independent model families and four
(impulsivity, acuity, mercuriality, yielding) do not; the two metatraits the coupling uses reproduce. The
remaining owed test is external, correlating the disposition read against a real personality instrument
that people have taken, which needs a labelled corpus none of ours yet holds.
