# ASHLAR SEVERE TEST PROTOCOL v1.0: frozen prospective human experiment

A ready to run severe test with no unresolved researcher discretion. The theory has reached the point of
having a predefined experiment capable of seriously hurting it. The test is frozen; what is needed is an
independent partner to run it. We are asking a partner to help **break** the theory, not endorse it.

## 1. The make or break question

> Given an independently measured person and experimentally assigned message geometry, does the frozen Ashlar
> coupling prospectively predict heterogeneous change in independently measured human belief or behaviour?

A powered, preregistered yes would be the first effect side evidence that the person by content geometry is
part of the machinery of persuasion, not only a description of language. A powered no would damage the strong
interpretation badly. That symmetry is the point.

## 2. Hypothesis and the frozen operator

The person side and content side instruments and the coupling are **frozen** at their current published form
(git tags `papers-v80-frozen` onward) and are not refitted in this experiment.

- Person side: Big Five measured before exposure, mapped by the frozen DeYoung recipe to the two DYNAMICS-8
  metatraits: plasticity = z(openness) + z(extraversion); stability = z(conscientiousness) + z(agreeableness)
  minus z(neuroticism).
- Content side: each message scored on the eight DYNAMICS-WEB axes by the frozen instrument, projected onto
  the frozen matter/manner PC1 and the originality axis.
- Frozen coupling score for a person i and message j, computed before any outcome is opened:
  `coupling_ij = plasticity_i * originality_j + stability_i * matter_manner_j`.

**H1 (directional):** coupling_ij positively moderates the treatment response: people move more when the
message geometry matches their disposition in the frozen coupling direction.

## 3. Design

A multi message randomised trial with repeated propositions. Each participant sees several independently
randomised items and never two versions of the same proposition, giving many person by message observations
without pretending they are independent. Analysis clusters by participant and by item.

- Propositions: a set of independent socio political or consumer propositions (final count fixed by the sample
  size section), each with the same factual core across arms.
- Arms per proposition: four to six messages that preserve the proposition but occupy different frozen
  DYNAMICS coordinates.
- Assignment: for each participant and each proposition shown, one arm is drawn at random.

## 4. Stimulus generation rules (no discretion at analysis time)

For every proposition, the arms are written to move in DYNAMICS space while holding constant: topic, the set of
factual claims, length band, readability band, source identity, formatting and the evidence set. Only the
character (matter versus manner balance, originality, affect, register and so on) is varied. Stimuli are
generated by a team that never sees outcomes. The realised DYNAMICS coordinates are then measured, not
assumed.

## 5. Manipulation checks (both required before the effect test is interpreted)

1. Independent scorers (human or the frozen instrument run blind) confirm each arm actually moved to its
   intended DYNAMICS coordinate, and that arms differ in the intended directions.
2. The realised experiment retains at least the between arm geometric variance assumed by the power
   simulation. If realised spread is below the locked minimum, the run is classified non adjudicative before
   outcomes are analysed.

## 6. Participants, recruitment, exclusions

- Recruitment: Prolific or CloudResearch representative sample, or a university or commercial research house
  panel. Sample size from section 10.
- Personality measured before any message exposure (short validated Big Five inventory).
- Exclusions, fixed in advance: failed attention checks, completion time below a floor, straight lining the
  personality inventory, duplicate participants. Exclusions applied before outcomes are opened.

## 7. Randomisation

Per participant and per proposition, arm assignment is drawn from a fixed seed schedule generated before
recruitment. The assignment log is frozen and stored with the preregistration.

## 8. Outcomes

- Primary: belief change, pre belief to post belief on each proposition (a fixed Likert scale), analysed as
  the change score.
- Behavioural secondary: one real endpoint per proposition where feasible, willingness to sign, choose,
  donate, share, or an incentivised choice. The behavioural endpoint is the stronger test and is preregistered
  as a co primary where the proposition allows it.

## 9. Analysis (the exact frozen pipeline)

For each participant message row: the frozen coupling scalar and the frozen content geometry are computed
before outcomes are opened. The model adds blocks in fixed order:

1. baseline: personality main effects, prior belief, arm and proposition controls, classical text measures.
2. plus a modern learned embedding of the message (the hard rival, as in the ArgQ and embedding contests).
3. plus the frozen content geometry main effects.
4. plus the frozen person by content interaction terms.

Headline: incremental out of sample R squared of block 4 over block 3, nested cross validation clustered by
participant and by item, bootstrap interval clustered by participant, permutation test shuffling the person to
message pairing. Effect direction checked against H1.

## 10. Power and sample size (simulation first): SET BY SIMULATION

The minimum N is decided by `cc_experiment_power_design.py`, which simulates this exact design through this
exact pipeline and finds the N that detects an injected Ashlar shaped effect of delta R squared = 0.01 at 80,
90 and 95 per cent power, for the between subjects design (one item per person) and the repeated measures
design (several items per person). The repeated measures economics are the point: many person by message
observations per participant sharply reduce the required number of people.

The first simulation (`EXPERIMENT_POWER.md`) returned a result that changes the protocol before any
recruitment: at the frozen +0.01 **held out absolute** threshold, power plateaus below 0.5 even at thousands
of participants (between subjects N = 4,000 gives 0.40; repeated measures N = 1,200 by six items gives 0.46),
because the held out incremental R squared is a downward biased estimator of a true 0.01 effect (mean about
0.0075). The binding constraint is the threshold and estimator, not sample size alone.

Two corrections follow, and the severe test adopts both:
1. **Design for a larger substantive effect.** Build the arms for genuinely large between arm DYNAMICS
   separation, targeting a coupling effect near 0.02, where the frozen form reaches about 0.87 power (0.99 at
   0.03). Manipulation strength buys more power than N.
2. **Score the pass rule on a less biased estimator.** Treat +0.01 as the population or in sample increment,
   with the held out increment for validation and a permutation test on the coupling coefficient as the
   significance gate.

With the corrected target and repeated measures, the required N is plausibly low thousands of participants; a
fresh N curve at the corrected target is the immediate pre registration step. At roughly four to five pounds
per participant on Prolific for a twenty minute study, that is single digit thousands of pounds up to about
fifteen thousand, set by the corrected simulation, not by budget.

N is chosen from the simulation, never from budget. The cost bracket follows from N and the design variant.

## 11. The frozen decision table

- **Pass**: frozen Ashlar interaction increment above +0.01 out of sample, bootstrap lower bound above zero,
  permutation p below the locked threshold, and direction matches H1. **Meaning:** first powered, prospective,
  effect side evidence that the geometry moderates real human change. The strong interpretation advances.
- **Weak or partial**: positive but below the +0.01 substantive threshold. **Meaning:** a real but small
  moderation; the geometry matters at the margin, not as a primary lever.
- **Fail**: a powered assay returns a near zero or opposite interaction. **Meaning:** the strong, effect side
  interpretation is damaged; the person by content bridge is likely descriptive and production side, not an
  effect side causal moderator.
- **Non adjudicative**: a manipulation check fails, or realised prospective power falls below the locked
  sensitivity threshold. **Meaning:** the run cannot vote; it contributes zero evidence either way (the exact
  status UCSC holds now).

## 12. Severity: double independent

One team generates stimuli without seeing outcomes; a second scores DYNAMICS blind; the outcome analysis runs
against this locked preregistration; ideally the final analysis is independently reproduced. This removes the
last routes by which a result could be shaped after the fact.

## 13. Design variants, ranked by cost (cheapest viable first)

1. **Repeated measures Prolific study, belief change only.** Cheapest viable; repeated propositions per
   participant reduce N. Sample size and cost from section 10.
2. **Repeated measures plus a behavioural endpoint.** Stronger; modest added cost per participant.
3. **University or external lab gold standard**, double independent, with an incentivised behavioural
   endpoint and independent reproduction. Strongest; highest cost.

## 14. What we are asking a partner to do

Fund and or run a defined experiment with a known sample requirement and a locked adjudication rule, not an
open ended research programme. The proposition to an academic or research partner is deliberately falsifying:

> We have frozen a test that could seriously hurt our own theory. Here is the exact design, the exact
> analysis, the sample size, and the rule that decides the outcome before any data is collected. Help us run
