=== Claude rater qualitative caveats (from reading the 24 texts) ===
1. Strong axis agreement: Claude vs 7B on matter/manner PC1 r=+0.714 (p=9e-5); commercial_drive r=+0.882; rigour
   r=+0.78, depth r=+0.79. The two families read the core axes the same way. Weak/negative agreement on register
   (r=-0.65), stance (0.16), candour (0.28) — the peripheral manner axes are rater-dependent.
2. The commercial_drive rise is substantially driven by DISPLAY ADVERTISING captured in the late-issue scan
   windows: Harper's 1930 and Popular Science 1961 first-6000-chars are mostly ad copy (Stetson hats, AC spark
   plugs, Lafayette catalogue). This is genuine commercialisation (the ads are part of the periodical) but it is
   partly a text-composition effect of where the bound-volume scan begins, not purely editorial-voice change.
3. Serious ARTICLE prose did NOT de-substantiate: BMJ 1960 (a densely referenced inflammation-biochemistry
   lecture) is the HIGHEST-matter item in the whole set. The BMJ 'fall' comes from the 1951 window carrying drug
   ads + contents, not from weak 1960 science. NAR and Contemporary Review held their substance too.
4. Honest synthesis: what falls is dominated by (a) mass-market magazines (Harper's, Popular Science) genuinely
   commercialising, and (b) ad/contents pages entering the sampled window. Cross-model CONFIRMS the direction
   (Claude sees matter fall in 6/6 titles; 7B 4/6; both agree the axis) and STRONGLY confirms commercialisation;
   it does NOT establish that serious-article editorial prose de-substantiated. A per-article-body extraction
   (strip ads/contents) is the clean next control.
